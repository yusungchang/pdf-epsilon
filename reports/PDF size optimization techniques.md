# Squeeze scans and images next, not text

*Research report for PDF Epsilon 0.3.0, 19 October 2026. Sources are given inline. Figures marked "project benchmark" are first-hand measurements made for this report on 11 real-world PDFs (53 MB in total, mostly Office and Keynote exports, Korean reports, white papers and one paper). They are not published numbers, and the sample is small.*

PDF Epsilon already covers the techniques that pay most on everyday files: resampling by drawn size, JPEG re-encoding, object and xref streams, dedup, pruning and font table dropping and subsetting. The biggest gains still open are in **images that are not photos**. Black-and-white scans can go to CCITT G4 or lossless JBIG2. Flat graphics can be stored with less data per pixel: gray instead of RGB, a palette, fewer bits, PNG predictors. JPEGs can get a lossless Huffman re-optimisation (typically **5–13%**). All of these are lossless, need no GPL code, and can be checked pixel for pixel before they are accepted. Below that tier are cheap, low-yield structural steps: a stronger deflate saves about **5% of non-image stream bytes**, which is only **1–3% of a whole file**. Then come the remaining font work (simple-font subsetting, merging font subsets, Type 1 to CFF) and opt-in removals that change behaviour. Removing the structure tree is the largest of those on tagged text documents, and it destroys accessibility. Several heavily marketed techniques fail the project's rules and should be skipped in 2026: Brotli, JPEG XL, WebP and AVIF output, lossy JBIG2, unembedding the base-14 fonts, linearisation and Ghostscript-style re-rendering. Content-stream rewriting and JPEG 2000 sit in between. They have real but modest payoffs, and their risk or encoder maturity argues for deferring them.

## Most remaining savings sit in non-photo images and JPEG entropy coding

Mainstream tools agree that **bilevel (1-bit) images** need their own codecs. OCRmyPDF converts every monochrome image to CCITT G4 at any optimisation level above zero, and to lossless JBIG2 when an encoder is installed ([OCRmyPDF optimizer](https://ocrmypdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/optimizer.html)). Ghostscript uses CCITTFax for mono images in every preset ([Ghostscript VectorDevices](https://ghostscript.readthedocs.io/en/latest/VectorDevices.html)). Apryse added CCITT in PDFNet 8.0, and a staff member then recommended it over JBIG2 ([PDFTron community](https://community.pdftron.com/t/how-do-i-use-optimizer-to-minimize-the-size-of-a-pdf/3533)). Published savings figures are dated rules of thumb. One survey gives roughly **15:1 for G4 and 21:1 for JBIG2** ([ResearchGate survey](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332539642_INTERNATIONAL_ITU-T_STANDARD_STILL_IMAGE_COMPRESSION_TECHNIQUES_FOR_GROUP_3_GROUP_4_CODED_IMAGES_--A_SURVEY)), and a typical 300 dpi page is about 75 KB under G4 ([USPTO 6748115](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/6748115)). The payoff only appears on scanned documents, but on those it dominates. Both encoders now exist as permissive pure-Rust crates. **`fax` 0.3.0 (MIT, about 65 M downloads)** encodes G4 ([docs.rs fax](https://docs.rs/crate/fax/latest)). **`jbig2enc-rust` 0.5.4 (MIT OR Apache-2.0)** has a lossless generic-region mode, but it has only 521 downloads, and its `symboldict` feature contains djvulibre-derived code that "may carry GPL implications" ([docs.rs jbig2enc-rust](https://docs.rs/crate/jbig2enc-rust/latest)). Symbol mode must never be used. It caused the 2013 Xerox incident, in which scanned floor plans had digits swapped (a 6 became an 8) ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/453236/xerox-scanners-found-to-sometimes-alter-numbers.html)). OCRmyPDF removed its lossy JBIG2 mode in v17 for exactly this reason ([OCRmyPDF v17 notes](https://ocrmypdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/releasenotes/version17.html)). A young encoder is acceptable because every output can be decoded again with `hayro-jbig2` (Apache-2.0 OR MIT) or `fax` and compared with the source bit for bit before it is used ([crates.io hayro-jbig2](https://crates.io/crates/hayro-jbig2)).

**Smaller pixel formats** are pure logic and lossless when exact. An RGB image whose pixels all have R=G=B becomes DeviceGray at a third of the raw size. An image with 256 colours or fewer becomes Indexed. A two-value image becomes 1 bpc, and then G4 applies. pdfsizeopt always runs sam2p for this, and its author describes the full search: try the smallest colour space and bit depth, try Flate with and without a predictor, try JBIG2, keep the smallest ([Szabó, EuroTeX 2009](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf); [pdfsizeopt](https://github.com/pts/pdfsizeopt)). The same paper adds two degenerate cases. An image of a single colour becomes a filled rectangle. A blocky image is stored at lower resolution and scaled back up through the CTM ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf)). PNG predictors (`/Predictor 15`, chosen per row) already sit on Epsilon's roadmap. No PDF-specific percentage has been published for them, so the project has to measure them on its own files ([pdfcpu filter docs](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/pdfcpu/pdfcpu/pkg/filter)). The filter-selection heuristics in `oxipng` (MIT) can be reused ([crates.io oxipng](https://crates.io/crates/oxipng)). These conversions have pitfalls: `/Decode` arrays, ICCBased spaces (gray detection must not drop a colour-managed RGB profile without care), and images that serve as `/SMask` or `/Mask`. Lossy palette quantisation belongs in the lossy presets only. The usual library, libimagequant, is GPL-3.0 ([docs.rs imagequant-sys](https://docs.rs/crate/imagequant-sys/latest)), so the permissive choice is `quantette` (MIT OR Apache-2.0) ([docs.rs quantette](https://docs.rs/quantette)).

**JPEG entropy coding** is the safest JPEG gain, and it applies to the Lossless preset because no pixel changes. Re-optimising Huffman tables and switching to progressive coding saved **5.42% (jpegtran) to 7.16% (mozjpeg's jpegtran)** on 920 web images. An earlier study found 13%, and progressive output is usually smaller once a file exceeds 10 KB ([Web Performance Calendar 2015](https://calendar.perfplanet.com/2015/upgrading-jpegtran-to-mozjpeg/)). On photo-heavy PDFs, where JPEG streams make up most of the bytes, that is the largest lossless whole-file gain left. The catch is that **no Rust crate does coefficient-level transcoding**. `zune-jpeg` decodes to pixels only, so Epsilon would need its own entropy decoder and re-encoder. That is bounded, well-specified work. Baseline output with optimised Huffman tables is the compatible default, because some minimal readers reject progressive (SOF2) data ([moonbitlang PR #606](https://github.com/moonbitlang/office.mbt/pull/606)). The same pass can strip EXIF and comment segments from embedded JPEGs, as Szabó recommends ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf)). It must keep the Adobe APP14 marker. Four-component JPEGs with APP14 are stored inverted and depend on a matching `/Decode [1 0 1 0 1 0 1 0]` ([unidoc pdfdebug PR #22](https://github.com/unidoc/pdfdebug/pull/22)). That also makes this the safest route for the planned CMYK support: coefficients and markers stay as they are, and converting to RGB would destroy print separations ([PeachPDF #1085](https://github.com/jhaygood86/PeachPDF/issues/1085)).

For the lossy presets, a better encoder adds a few percent at equal quality. mozjpeg averaged **about 5% smaller than libjpeg-turbo** ([Mozilla](https://blog.mozilla.org/mozilla/improving-jpeg-image-encoding/)), and the pure-Rust **`mozjpeg-rs` (BSD-3-Clause)** brings trellis quantisation ([crates.io mozjpeg-rs](https://crates.io/crates/mozjpeg-rs)). Google's jpegli claims up to 35% at high quality ([Google Open Source Blog](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2024/04/introducing-jpegli-new-jpeg-coding-library.html)). That claim is disputed at medium quality ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39957010)), and the pure-Rust ports `jpegli-rs` and `zenjpeg` are **AGPL**, so they are out ([crates.io jpegli-rs](https://crates.io/crates/jpegli-rs)). JPEG 2000 compresses somewhat better than JPEG ([bibwild](https://bibwild.wordpress.com/2023/07/18/investigating-ocr-and-text-pdfs-from-digital-collections/)). But re-encoding an existing JPEG adds a second lossy generation, the only pure-Rust encoder (`dicom-toolkit-jpeg2000`) has about 15k downloads ([docs.rs](https://docs.rs/dicom-toolkit-jpeg2000)), and older software may not read JPXDecode ([iText KB](https://kb.itextpdf.com/itext/which-pdf-filters-are-used-to-encode-data)). MRC splits a colour scan into a 1-bit text mask and a low-resolution background. It reaches reported ratios of **up to 10×** ([mrcpdf](https://github.com/msmarkgu/mrcpdf)) and **about 250:1 on one page** ([archive-pdf-tools](https://github.com/internetarchive/archive-pdf-tools)). It rewrites page content and depends on segmentation quality, so it belongs in a later, explicit "scanned document" mode.

## Better deflate and structural tidying are cheap, safe and small

Recompressing every non-image stream harder is nearly free to build. Its gains are real but limited. In the project benchmark, the non-image streams (18.2 MB raw) came to 5.89 MB at the engine's current zlib level 9. **libdeflate level 12 saved 5.02% overall (6.5% on content streams, 2.7% on fonts) in 3.6× the time. Zopfli saved 5.83% in 260× the time** (164 s against 0.63 s). miniz_oxide at "level 10" gained nothing. Because images usually dominate a PDF, the whole-file effect is about 1–3%. The ranking matches published figures: Zopfli output is typically 3–8% smaller than zlib's maximum, and qpdf, which added it in 11.10, calls it about 100× slower ([qpdf README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/qpdf/qpdf@main/README.md); [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/compress-data-more-densely-with-zopfli/)). The licensing is clean. `zopfli` is Apache-2.0 and pure Rust ([crates.io](https://crates.io/crates/zopfli)). `libdeflater` is Apache-2.0 over MIT C code, so it needs a C compiler at build time. That is acceptable for a Tauri build, but strictly it is not pure Rust. The same pass should also recompress Flate image streams, which the benchmark did not measure.

The rest of the structural work comes from pdfsizeopt's 2009 list, and each item is lossless if canonicalisation respects PDF semantics. **Equivalence-class dedup** collapses duplicate objects that byte comparison cannot, including cyclic ones such as `/Next`/`/Prev` pairs. **Decoded-content stream dedup** treats two streams as equal when their decoded bytes match, ignoring `/Filter` and `/DecodeParms`. This catches the per-image copies of the same ICC profile that exporters write. **Pixel-value image dedup** finds images that are identical after decoding, even when one is stored as RGB and the other as Indexed. Smaller steps include decoding ASCIIHex, ASCII85, LZW and RLE into Flate, sorting objects by type inside object streams so similar objects share the 32 KB deflate window, a TIFF-predictor xref stream, dense renumbering, and dropping default-valued keys and `/ProcSet` ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf); [cpdf manual](https://coherentpdf.com/cpdfmanual/cpdfmanualch5.html)). Szabó's measurements show where these pay off: the "other" (non-stream objects) and xref columns shrank by **77–96% and 87–100%**. Those numbers also include object streams, which Epsilon already writes, so the gain still available on modern files is unknown and probably small. **Unused-resource pruning** removes resource names that no content stream references, so the existing garbage collector can then delete fonts and images that pages inherited but never draw. This is common after splitting or merging. It needs only a tokenizer scan of `Tf`, `Do`, `gs`, `cs`, `sh` and `BDC`, and it should give up on any parse failure ([qpdf CLI](https://qpdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/cli.html)). A full rewrite already drops superseded versions from incremental updates. Epsilon's analyzer detects signatures, and a rewrite invalidates them ([qpdf CLI](https://qpdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/cli.html)).

**Content-stream rewriting** can save more, but at higher risk. In the project benchmark, a lossless lexer pass (whitespace, comments, `0.5`→`.5`) cut the deflated size of content streams by **3.7%**. Rounding numbers to 3 decimals reached 6.9%, and to 2 decimals 11.2%. These are upper bounds, because the output was not render-checked. pdfsizeopt with Multivalent measured −1% to −20% on content streams, with large gains only on Inkscape output ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf)). The obstacle is lopdf. Its content decoder still mishandles inline images (issue #78, open), and it once silently dropped all operators after a comment ([lopdf #78](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/issues/78); [lopdf #535](https://github.com/J-F-Liu/lopdf/issues/535)). A safe version needs its own lexer that copies strings and inline-image data verbatim. Rounding is lossy: a large `cm` scale amplifies the error, so precision must follow the CTM and the step belongs in lossy presets only. q/Q elimination, operator merging and off-page culling need a full graphics-state interpreter, and the literature gives no measured gains for them.

## Font work left is producer-specific and pays in bursts

Most producers already subset fonts: TeX drivers, Ghostscript, Word, LibreOffice and Chrome do it by default ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf)). Font savings therefore come in rare, large bursts, like Epsilon's 15.2 MB → 80 KB Arial Unicode case, rather than steadily. Three gaps remain beyond the planned simple-font subsetting. **Type 1 to CFF conversion** is lossless. Szabó gives examples of **200 kB → 50 kB** on short documents and −84% of font bytes on one test file, because CFF is binary and Type 1's eexec encryption defeats Flate ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf)). The value is limited to older TeX and dvips output. Every existing implementation is GPL or AGPL (pdfsizeopt, Ghostscript) or Python (fontTools). Rust has only Type 1 parsers (`hayro-font`, Apache-2.0 OR MIT), so this is a multi-week job, including hint translation. **Merging font subsets** helps PDFs that were concatenated from several sources. iText measured −45% on a small file with 4 subsets of one font ([iText KB](https://kb.itextpdf.com/itext/smarter-font-handling-in-itext-pdfoptimizer-consol)), and pdfsizeopt's font column fell 91% on a proceedings volume ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf)). The general case means re-encoding every text string, and pdfsizeopt's own README advises turning the option off when fonts break ([pdfsizeopt](https://github.com/pts/pdfsizeopt)). One special case is safe and matches Epsilon's design: Identity-H subsets with retained glyph IDs, the same base font and byte-identical outlines wherever both subsets have a glyph. Those can share one FontFile2 without touching any content stream. **Pruning unused CFF subroutines** after subsetting is fontTools' default behaviour ([fontTools subset](https://fonttools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/subset/index.html)). It needs a charstring interpreter. Rust has no implementation, and skera's desubroutinize flag is still marked unimplemented ([skera](https://github.com/googlefonts/fontations/tree/main/skera)).

For the planned simple-font and CFF subsetting, the crate situation is as follows. **`subsetter` (typst, MIT OR Apache-2.0)** is the most mature, but it always renumbers glyph IDs and removes `cmap`, so it suits only CID fonts whose text is re-encoded ([typst/subsetter](https://github.com/typst/subsetter)). **`skera` 0.8.0 (fontations, MIT OR Apache-2.0)** has a `RETAIN_GIDS` flag, which fits Epsilon's keep-the-IDs approach, but its CFF coverage is unverified. A custom CFF pass that replaces unused charstrings with a bare `endchar` keeps the INDEX count, and therefore every glyph ID, while shrinking the data. Simple TrueType fonts must keep their `cmap` unchanged, because viewers disagree on the (3,0)/(3,1)/(1,0) lookup rules ([Adobe community](https://community.adobe.com/questions-94/symbol-truetype-font-with-multiple-code-ranges-1503402)). They should also keep `post` glyph names when there is no ToUnicode ([fontTools subset](https://fonttools.readthedocs.io/en/latest/subset/index.html)). **Unembedding the base-14 fonts** saves tens of KB, but viewers then substitute different glyph shapes, PDF/A forbids it, and pdfsizeopt's author rules it out ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf); [Mapsoft](https://mapsoft.com/posts/pdf-pdfa-standard.html)). It fails "never risk content".

## Opt-in removals win big on tagged files but change what the document does

The largest single structural item in competitor benchmarks is removing a feature, not compressing anything. In LibreOffice bug 157028, one document was **3.68 MB with tags and 0.63 MB without** ([LibreOffice bug 157028](https://www.libreoffice.org/bugzilla/show_bug.cgi?id=157028)). In Lyonite's 2026 test, much of iLovePDF's −44.6% on a 62k-tag IRS publication came from deleting tags, while lossless qpdf grew the same file slightly. iLovePDF, Smallpdf and every Ghostscript preset deleted all tags in every tagged file ([Lyonite](https://lyonite.com/tested/compression-fidelity)). Lyonite sells a competing product, but its third-party measurements are detailed and consistent. Acrobat, qpdf (`--remove-structure`) and mutool (`--structure=drop`) all offer tag removal as an explicit option ([qpdf CLI](https://qpdf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/cli.html); [mutool clean](https://mupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tools/mutool-clean.html)). Epsilon should do the same: off in every preset, labelled as destroying accessibility and PDF/UA, and refused on PDF/A files. The analyzer already reads `pdfaid:part`. Other candidates are safe only under conditions. **XFA packets** can be dropped when `NeedsRendering` is false and every AcroForm widget has an appearance ([Aspose XFA](https://docs.aspose.com/pdf/net/xfa-forms/)). **Image `/Alternates`** and **Web Capture data** are informational. JavaScript, embedded files, hidden layers, named destinations (external `file.pdf#name` links break) and form flattening all change behaviour, so they belong behind explicit switches ([Szabó](https://ntg.nl/maps/39/15.pdf); [cpdf manual](https://coherentpdf.com/cpdfmanual/cpdfmanualch5.html)).

The newer standards do not help yet. The Brotli extension (`/BrotliDecode`) is claimed to give 10–25% smaller files, but a community test of 55 files found only **4.5%**, and Acrobat Reader could not open such files as of January 2026 ([heise](https://heise.de/-11151618); [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/4124626/new-pdf-compression-filter-will-save-space-need-software-updates-2.html)). JPEG XL is moving into PDF 2.0 through a draft amendment, but publication is unconfirmed and viewers do not support it ([ISO 89640](https://www.iso.org/standard/89640.html); [heise](https://heise.de/-11073527)). WebP and AVIF have no PDF standardisation path. Linearisation makes files slightly larger ([Ghostscript docs](https://ghostscript.readthedocs.io/en/latest/VectorDevices.html)). Ghostscript re-rendering made files bigger on average (−16.47%), emptied form fields and lost 4.2% of one paper's text ([Lyonite](https://lyonite.com/tested/compression-fidelity)). That confirms Epsilon's approach of editing objects in place rather than re-rendering. Lyonite also measured qpdf's lossless pass at **7.07%** ([Lyonite](https://lyonite.com/tested/compression-fidelity)), and Epsilon already does what that pass does. A further lossless step should be expected to add single-digit percentages, except on scans, fully embedded fonts and tagged documents.

## Recommended roadmap for PDF Epsilon

**1. Bilevel codecs: CCITT G4 now, lossless JBIG2 behind a check.** First, detect true 1-bit images, and 8-bit images that contain only two values. Encode them with `fax` (MIT) into `/CCITTFaxDecode` with `/K -1`, decode the result again, compare bit for bit, and keep it only if smaller. Then add `jbig2enc-rust` (MIT OR Apache-2.0) in generic-region mode with `symboldict` disabled, checking each result with `hayro-jbig2` (Apache-2.0 OR MIT). *Payoff:* the largest available gain on scanned and faxed documents, often several-fold for bilevel images that are now Flate-encoded. *Effort:* small for G4 (about a week including edge cases such as BlackIs1 and odd widths); medium for JBIG2. *Risk:* low with the round-trip check. Lossless, so it is allowed in the Lossless preset.

**2. Lossless image representation and the planned PNG predictors.** Use exact gray detection, palette detection (256 colours or fewer becomes Indexed), bit-depth packing and degenerate-image detection (a single colour becomes a filled rectangle). Then try Flate with no predictor and with `/Predictor 15` per-row filters, and keep the smallest, as pdfsizeopt does. Leave any image with an unusual `/Decode`, an ICC space that cannot be preserved, or a mask role untouched. *Crates:* none required. `oxipng` (MIT) can serve as a reference for filter heuristics. *Payoff:* medium and broad (screenshots, charts, UI captures in slide decks). It feeds item 1, because a detected 1-bit image then gets G4. *Effort:* medium. *Risk:* low.

**3. Lossless JPEG Huffman optimisation and metadata stripping.** Write a DCT coefficient decoder and re-encoder that builds optimal Huffman tables. Emit baseline by default, with progressive as an option. Drop EXIF, XMP and COM segments, keep APP14, and leave `/Decode` unchanged. This also gives CMYK JPEGs a safe path without re-encoding. *Crates:* none suitable (`zune-jpeg` decodes only to pixels). Custom code, with `zune-jpeg` (MIT/Apache/Zlib) used to verify that the decoded pixels are identical. *Payoff:* 5–13% of JPEG bytes, the largest lossless gain on photo-heavy PDFs, which are the most common large files. *Effort:* medium (2–3 weeks). *Risk:* low with the pixel-identity check.

**4. Stronger deflate and a structural pack.** Make `libdeflater` level 12 (Apache-2.0, bundles MIT C libdeflate) the re-deflate path for all Flate streams, images included, and keep a stream's original encoding when the new one is not smaller. Offer `zopfli` (Apache-2.0, pure Rust) only as a "maximum" setting or for small streams. In the same release, add decoded-content stream dedup (ICC profiles), ASCIIHex/ASCII85/LZW/RLE to Flate, unused-resource pruning with a tokenizer scan, object sorting within object streams, and `/ProcSet` removal. *Payoff:* about 5% of non-image bytes, 1–3% of a typical file, and more on merged files. *Effort:* small to medium. *Risk:* very low. If the C build dependency is unacceptable, `zopfli` on streams under 1 MB gets most of the ratio in pure Rust, at a time cost.

**5. Finish font subsetting and add safe subset merging.** Complete the planned simple TrueType, Type1C and CFF subsetting while keeping glyph IDs. For TrueType use `skera` with `RETAIN_GIDS`, or a custom glyf/loca pass on `read-fonts`/`write-fonts` (all MIT OR Apache-2.0). For CFF, a custom pass replaces unused charstrings with `endchar`. Keep `cmap`, and keep `post` names when there is no ToUnicode. Then merge Identity-H subsets with retained GIDs whose outlines are byte-identical, and prune unused CFF subroutines. *Payoff:* rare but very large per file (MB to KB), and meaningful on concatenated volumes. *Effort:* medium to large. *Risk:* medium, contained by the existing AcroForm `/DR` exclusion and the render and text comparison.

**6. Opt-in "remove accessibility tags" and other removals.** Add a clearly labelled option, off in every preset, that removes `/StructTreeRoot`, `/MarkInfo` and the ParentTree (refused on PDF/A, with a warning about PDF/UA). It should also drop XFA when the AcroForm fallback is complete, plus image `/Alternates` and Web Capture data. *Crates:* lopdf only. *Payoff:* up to about 80% on heavily tagged text documents. Without this option, online tools will keep winning those comparisons. *Effort:* small. *Risk:* none to rendering, but it changes function, so it must stay a user choice.

## Conclusion

Epsilon has already picked the easy structural wins. What remains splits into two kinds of work. Codec work (G4, JBIG2, JPEG entropy coding, pixel formats) is bounded and lossless, it can be checked pixel for pixel, and permissive Rust crates now exist for it, mostly published in 2026. Policy work (tags, XFA, removals) is trivial to code but changes what the document does. Competitors' headline numbers mostly come from that second category, done silently. Epsilon can match them honestly by offering it as an explicit opt-in.

Three numbers are still unknown and should be measured on the project's own files before committing to the order above: how much PNG predictors and pixel-format reduction save on real slide decks, how lossless JBIG2 compares with G4 on modern scans, and how often merged or fully embedded fonts occur. The analyzer's existing per-category byte accounting can measure all three without new tooling. Running it over a larger set of real files is the cheapest next step.
